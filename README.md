<section id="bwb" class="case-study">
  <header class="case-study-header">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow dark">FLAGSHIP AIRCRAFT PROJECT · JAN–APR 2026</p>
      <h2>Blended-Wing-Body Aerodynamics</h2>
      <p class="case-study-subtitle">
        Geometry design · CFD solution verification · FDM fabrication · force-balance test · tuft flow visualization
      </p>
    </div>
    <a class="text-link" href="assets/BWB_Project_Showcase_Tufts%20(4).html">Full technical showcase ↗</a>
  </header>

  <div class="case-hero">
    <img src="assets/IMG_3685 (1).jpeg"
         alt="3D-printed BWB model mounted to a three-component wind tunnel balance">
    <div class="case-hero-caption">
      <span>EWT 18 × 18 in test section</span>
      <span>5.0% blockage</span>
      <span>V∞ ≈ 40 mph</span>
      <span>−2° → 20° test sweep</span>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="metric-grid four case-metrics">
    <div class="metric"><strong>7.31</strong><span>peak CFD L/D at 6°</span></div>
    <div class="metric"><strong>14–16°</strong><span>stall onset in tunnel data</span></div>
    <div class="metric"><strong>3.36 N</strong><span>peak normal force at 18°</span></div>
    <div class="metric"><strong>&lt;0.5%</strong><span>domain sensitivity in CL/CD</span></div>
  </div>

  <p class="case-lede-single">
    I designed a custom blended-wing-body outer mold line in SOLIDWORKS, established a repeatable pre-stall CFD workflow,
    fabricated a 1:1 FDM model, and extended the physical test campaign through 20° angle of attack to characterize the stall sequence.
  </p>

  <div class="case-section">
    <div class="case-section-copy">
      <p class="section-number">01 / GEOMETRY</p>
      <h3>Designed the outer mold line from cross-sections to test article</h3>
      <p>
        A multi-section loft with four guide curves blends a custom centerbody into the outer panels.
        The geometry was carried directly from CAD into simulation, FDM fabrication, and wind-tunnel testing.
      </p>
    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="case-section">
    <div class="case-section-copy">
      <p class="section-number">02 / CFD SOLUTION VERIFICATION</p>
      <h3>Checked numerical sensitivity before using the CFD sweep for design conclusions</h3>
      <p>
        Steady external-flow cases covered −2° to +8° at 40 mph. A domain study at 4° changed CL by 0.17%
        and CD by 0.43%; medium-to-fine mesh refinement changed CL by 1.25% and CD by 0.37%.
      </p>

  <div class="case-section">
    <div class="case-section-copy">
      <p class="section-number">03 / WIND-TUNNEL STALL CAMPAIGN</p>
      <h3>Extended the experiment 12° beyond the CFD range to capture stall</h3>
      <p>
        The tunnel sweep ran from −2° through 20° using a three-component balance, wind-off tare,
        point-by-point dynamic-pressure correction, and 25 Hz acquisition. Stall onset appears around 14–16°;
        normal force peaks at 3.36 N at 18° and falls only about 1% by 20°.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="finding">
      <span>02</span>

<section id="rotorcraft" class="case-study compact-case">
  <header class="case-study-header">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow dark">RESEARCH · FLIGHT DYNAMICS</p>
      <h2>Learned Rotorcraft Dynamics</h2>
      <p class="case-study-subtitle">
        Physics-informed ML · rigid-body dynamics · rollout stability · controller-facing evaluation
      </p>
    </div>
    <span class="status-pill light">IEEE Aerospace 2027 · Accepted</span>
  </header>

  <div class="split-feature rotorcraft-feature">
    <div>
      <p class="project-lede">
        I am developing learned rotorcraft models that predict forces and moments while preserving the rigid-body equations of motion.
        My work focuses on whether the models remain stable and useful when rolled forward through complete trajectories,
        not only whether they minimize one-step prediction error.
      </p>

  <ul class="clean-list">
    <li>Built the PyTorch flight-dynamics and force/moment integration pipeline.</li>
    <li>Compared feed-forward, temporal, recurrent, and training-objective variants under matched conditions.</li>
    <li>Evaluated trajectory drift, stability, tracking, and control-relevant response on held-out cases.</li>
  </ul>

  <p class="paper-note">
    <strong>Accepted paper:</strong>
    “From Accurate Predictions to Controller-Facing Learned Rotorcraft Surrogates: Stability &amp; Tracking.”
  </p>
</div>
