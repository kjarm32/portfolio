Section 01

Geometry & Design

SOLIDWORKS multi-section loft with guide curves and cross-sectional profiles defining the BWB outer mold line.
Rendered SOLIDWORKS model. Span 8 in (0.203 m), length 9 in (0.229 m), peak thickness 2 in.

The outer mold line was defined using a multi-section loft with four guide curves spanning centerbody to wingtip. The centerbody profile is a modified NACA 00-series section with an added volume term for blending and packaging, maintaining a smooth leading edge and tapered trailing edge. Sref = 9.62 × 10−3 m² (top-view planform silhouette), c̄ = 0.047 m.

Section 04

Key Findings

FINDING 01
Stall is gradual — CFD did not predict it

The force-balance sweep extends 12° beyond the CFD range and captures a progressive stall onset at 14–16° identified by two independent indicators: normal-force slope dropping below 50% of linear-regime reference and pitching-moment peak reversal at 16°. No sudden CL break — consistent with a blended, low-Re planform delaying abrupt leading-edge separation.

FINDING 02
CFD peak efficiency confirmed at 6° AoA

Verified L/D peak of 7.31 at 6° with numerically credible methodology: convergence + 100 stability iterations; domain sensitivity <0.5%; mesh medium→fine ΔCL ≈ 1.25%. Results are repeatable and reported with consistent post-processing (fixed Cp scale, matched VX cut-planes) — not just decorative visuals.

FINDING 03
PM peak leads NF peak — AC shift near stall

Pitching moment peaks at 16° while normal force peaks at 18°. This 2° lead indicates the aerodynamic center is shifting forward near stall — a physical signature of outer-panel separation preceding the final lift peak. This detail is recoverable only with simultaneous PM measurement, not force-only testing.

FINDING 04
Zero-lift angle ≈ −2° — confirms cambered centerbody

Normal force at 0° AoA = 0.361 N (non-zero). The zero-lift angle is approximately −1.5° to −2°, consistent with the NACA-derived cambered centerbody producing positive loading at zero incidence — the design intent. Provides a geometric cross-check on the CFD result where CL(0°) = +0.059.

Section 04 — Tuft Flow Visualization

Surface Flow Patterns Across the Stall

Lightweight yarn tufts (~7 mm, ~12% chord spacing) were attached to the upper surface of the BWB model and recorded overhead at each AoA via a fixed top-down camera. The turntable degree markings visible in each frame provide a direct AoA reference. Tufts lie flat when flow is attached, deflect laterally in transitional zones, and reverse or flutter chaotically during separation — making the spatial stall pattern directly readable frame by frame.

Key result: Separation initiates at the centerbody and progresses outboard with increasing AoA. The outer wing panels remain partially attached well past centerbody stall onset — a physically meaningful result for BWB configurations where the centerbody carries significant chord-wise loading.

Region 1 — Pre-stall (~−2° to +4°). Tufts lie flat and stream rearward uniformly across the full planform, centerbody to wingtip. No curl, no lateral deflection, no separation. Clean attached flow baseline confirmed across all three chordwise rows.
FINDING — PRE-STALL
Fully attached flow confirmed to at least +4°

All tufts aligned rearward with no visible lateral bias. The uniformity across span is consistent with the near-linear CL slope seen in CFD over this range. The centerbody tufts in particular show no leading-edge perturbation — suction is building but separation has not initiated.

CFD context: CL increases from −0.041 at −2° to +0.161 at +2°. L/D rising steeply. No adverse pressure gradient strong enough to trigger separation at this stage.
FINDING — TRANSITION / ONSET
Centerbody tufts begin curling at ~6°–10°

Tufts over the centerbody upper surface show slight lateral deflection and minor curl near the leading-edge junction. Wingtip tufts remain cleanly aligned. This spatial asymmetry — centerbody perturbed, outer panel clean — is the earliest physical signal of the stall sequence. The centerbody is generating stronger suction and is the first region to approach separation.

Force data context: normal-force slope is still near its linear reference value at 8°–10°. The tuft perturbation is visible before the force balance detects slope loss — tufts are a leading indicator here.
Region 2 — Transition / Onset (~6° to 10°). Centerbody tufts show lateral curl near the leading-edge junction while outer-panel tufts remain attached. Inboard-first perturbation visible. Turntable markings confirm AoA. Wingtip tufts still streaming cleanly rearward.
Region 3 — Stall Onset (~14° to 16°). Tufts over the centerbody visibly disordered and lifting off the surface — incipient separation confirmed. Outer-panel tufts show deflection but are not fully reversed. The separated zone is clearly inboard-concentrated at this stage.
FINDING — STALL ONSET
Clear centerbody separation at 14–16°, outer panel still partially attached

This frame is the most important in the sequence. Centerbody tufts are disordered and lifting — separated flow confirmed visually. Outer-panel tufts show deflection but remain partially attached. This matches both indicators from the force-balance data: the normal-force slope dropping below 50% of the linear-regime reference and the pitching-moment reversal at 16°. The two independent measurements agree on the same AoA window.

Force data: PM peaks at 16° then reverses — aerodynamic center shifting forward. NF slope loss exceeds 50% of linear reference. Both indicators fire at the same AoA the tufts show visible centerbody separation.
FINDING — POST-STALL
Widespread centerbody separation by 18–20°, wingtips persist

Large separated zone confirmed across the centerbody. Some tufts reversed. Wingtip tufts remain partially ordered — the outer panel has not fully stalled. This spatial pattern explains the gradual character of the stall in the force data: the BWB is shedding lift progressively from the center outward, not collapsing suddenly across the full span. The soft 0.034 N NF drop from 18° to 20° is consistent with partial spanwise separation, not a full-span event.

Force data: peak NF = 3.36 N at 18°, drops to 3.33 N at 20° — a 1% decline consistent with partial, not total, separation. Wingtip lift contribution persisting explains the absence of an abrupt stall break.
Region 4 — Post-stall (~18° to 20°). Widespread tuft disruption across the centerbody with some reversal visible. Wingtip tufts partially ordered. Separated zone clearly concentrated inboard. This inboard-first, progressive separation pattern explains the soft stall character in the force data.
TUFT + FORCE DATA — COMBINED INTERPRETATION

Tuft flow visualization and force-balance data tell a consistent story: separation initiates at the centerbody near 14–16° and progresses outboard, while the outer wing panels remain partially attached through 20°. The inboard-first separation sequence explains why the stall is gradual rather than abrupt — the BWB geometry distributes the load-shedding across a 6° range rather than collapsing suddenly. This is physically meaningful for a blended planform and would not have been recoverable from force data alone without the spatial context the tufts provide.

Portfolio / Resume Bullets

Project Summary

  • PIPELINE Executed a full aerodynamics design-to-test pipeline on a custom blended-wing-body (BWB) — SOLIDWORKS multi-section loft, validated steady-state CFD (SolidWorks Flow Simulation), FDM fabrication, and EWT three-component force-balance wind tunnel testing — all at matched Re ≈ 5.5 × 10⁴ with no scale correction required.
  • CFD Characterized pre-stall performance via validated CFD across −2° to +8° AoA (40 mph): peak L/D ≈ 7.3 at 6°, CL increasing near-linearly; confirmed numerically with <0.5% domain sensitivity and medium→fine ΔCL ≈ 1.25%; reported with consistent post-processing (fixed Cp scale, identical VX cut-planes across AoA).
  • WIND TUNNEL Extended the sweep to 20° AoA in wind tunnel testing, capturing a gradual stall the CFD range did not reach — identified onset at 14–16° using dual indicators (normal-force slope collapse and pitching-moment reversal), measured peak NF of 3.36 N at 18°, and confirmed gradual separation behavior consistent with a low-Re blended planform.
  • METHODOLOGY Designed a rigorous multi-run balance campaign with wind-off tare procedure (WOT), tuft-on and tuft-free configurations, and point-by-point dynamic-pressure correction at 25 Hz / 25 samples per point — producing clean normal force, axial force, and pitching moment across a 12-point sweep with tunnel dynamic pressure stable within ±1.5 Pa.
